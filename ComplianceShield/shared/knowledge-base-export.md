## 2. Florida Construction Regulatory Landscape

### 2.1 OSHA (Federal) — Workplace Safety

Florida is a federal OSHA state, meaning federal standards under Title 29 of the Code of Federal Regulations (29 CFR), Part 1926 (Construction), apply directly.

- **Fall Protection (29 CFR 1926.501–503):** The #1 cited violation in construction nationally. Any work at 6 feet or above requires fall protection systems. Most small contractors are non-compliant.
- **Scaffolding (29 CFR 1926.451):** The #2 most cited. Competent person requirements, guardrail specs, load ratings.
- **Hazard Communication (29 CFR 1926.59):** Safety Data Sheets (SDS) must be accessible for every chemical on-site. Written HazCom program required.
- **Electrical (29 CFR 1926.405):** Ground-Fault Circuit Interrupter (GFCI) protection, proper wiring methods on temporary power.
- **Trenching & Excavation (29 CFR 1926.650–652):** Any trench 5+ feet deep requires protective systems. Cave-in deaths trigger immediate OSHA investigations.
- **Recordkeeping (29 CFR 1904):** OSHA 300 Log required for firms with 11+ employees. Many small contractors don't know this threshold exists.

### 2.2 DBPR / CILB — Florida Contractor Licensing

The Construction Industry Licensing Board (CILB) under DBPR governs all contractor licensing in Florida.

- **License categories:** Certified (statewide) vs. Registered (county-specific). Operating outside license scope is a criminal misdemeanor under Florida Statute 489.127.
- **Continuing Education (CE):** 14 hours per biennium, including 1 hour of workplace safety, 1 hour of business practices, 1 hour of workers' comp, and 1 hour of wind mitigation (unique to Florida).
- **Financial responsibility:** Net worth requirements, credit history checks, and proof of insurance are required at renewal.
- **Qualifying agent rules:** The qualifier must exercise direct supervision. If the qualifier leaves, the company has 60 days to replace them or cease operations.
- **Permit pulling:** Only properly licensed contractors may pull building permits in Florida. Unlicensed activity triggers automatic $5,000 fines per offense.

### 2.3 Workers' Compensation

- **Threshold:** Construction employers with 1 or more employees (including the owner) must carry workers' compensation. This is lower than most states.
- **Class code audits:** Insurers audit payroll annually. Misclassifying workers under a lower-risk code triggers premium back-charges plus penalties.
- **Stop-work orders:** Florida's Division of Workers' Compensation actively issues stop-work orders to uninsured contractors. The penalty is $1,000/day plus 2x the evaded premium for the preceding 2-year period.
- **Subcontractor compliance:** General contractors are liable for uninsured subcontractors' workers' comp claims (Florida Statute 440.10). If a sub lacks coverage, the general contractor is on the hook.

### 2.4 EPA & Environmental

- **Lead Renovation, Repair and Painting (RRP) Rule:** Any work on pre-1978 buildings that disturbs lead paint requires an EPA-certified firm and trained renovators. Fines up to $37,500/day per violation.
- **Stormwater / NPDES:** Construction sites disturbing 1+ acres require a Stormwater Pollution Prevention Plan (SWPPP) and NPDES permit through the Florida Department of Environmental Protection (FDEP).
- **Asbestos NESHAP:** Demolition and renovation of commercial buildings requires an asbestos inspection before work begins.

### 2.5 Florida Building Code & Hurricane Compliance

- **Florida Building Code (FBC):** Updated on a 3-year cycle (current 8th Edition, effective 12/31/2023). Contractors must comply with the current edition for all permitted work.
- **High-Velocity Hurricane Zone (HVHZ):** Miami-Dade and Broward counties have enhanced wind-load requirements beyond the standard FBC. Product approvals (Notice of Acceptance, NOA) required for materials used in the HVHZ.
- **Energy code:** Florida has adopted energy efficiency requirements that add insulation, HVAC, and fenestration compliance obligations.

### 2.6 Employment & Tax Compliance

- **Employee vs. Subcontractor Classification:** Florida follows IRS common-law rules for classification. Misclassification triggers back taxes, penalties, and potential fraud charges.
- **E-Verify:** Florida requires public employers and their contractors to use E-Verify for employment eligibility verification (effective July 2023 under SB 1718). Private employers with 25+ employees must also comply.
- **Sales tax on materials:** Contractors are generally the end consumers of building materials and must pay sales tax. Mishandling this triggers Department of Revenue audits.

## Domain summary for checklist derivation (Phase 1)

Six domains, matching the Playbook's Phase 1 spec:

1. **OSHA (29 CFR 1926)** — fall protection, scaffolding, HazCom/SDS, electrical/GFCI, trenching/excavation, OSHA 300 recordkeeping (11+ employees).
2. **DBPR / CILB Licensing** — license category/scope match, CE hours (14/biennium incl. wind mitigation), financial responsibility at renewal, qualifying agent coverage, permit-pulling eligibility.
3. **Workers' Compensation** — coverage at 1+ employees, exemption filings, class code accuracy, subcontractor coverage verification (GC liability under F.S. 440.10).
4. **EPA / Environmental** — RRP firm + renovator certification (pre-1978 structures), SWPPP/NPDES for 1+ acre disturbance, asbestos inspection before demo/reno.
5. **Florida Building Code / HVHZ** — current FBC edition compliance, HVHZ product approvals (NOA) in Miami-Dade/Broward, energy code compliance.
6. **Employment & Tax** — worker classification (IRS common-law test), E-Verify compliance (25+ employees / public contractors), sales tax handling on materials.

Risk severity weighting note (from Section 1.1/8 of the source doc): OSHA and Workers' Comp violations carry the highest immediate dollar/shutdown exposure (stop-work orders, per-day fines) and should weight heaviest in the overall risk score; Building Code and Employment/Tax gaps tend to surface on audit/renewal cycles rather than immediate site shutdowns and can weight moderate.
